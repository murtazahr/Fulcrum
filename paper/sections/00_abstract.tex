\begin{abstract}
Federated learning protects raw data by sharing only model updates,
yet the topology that orchestrates these updates is itself an
attack surface that existing privacy mechanisms do not address.
We study a passive adversary who knows the network topology and the
organisational labelling of clients, observes per-round parameter
updates, and seeks to recover each client's sensitive-class concentration.
Two contributions follow. First, we introduce \TADI, a topology-aware
distributional inference attack whose four channel ablations isolate the
marginal information value of parameter observations, structural
position, and organisational membership. Second, we prove a per-client
mutual-information bound that decomposes additively into a controllable
mechanism term (the standard \mbox{DP-SGD} contribution) and an
uncontrollable prior-coupling floor; we then derive a closed-form,
balanced min-max optimal noise allocation that strictly dominates uniform
\mbox{DP-SGD} whenever the structural-leverage scores are non-uniform.
We instantiate the leverage abstraction through three principled proxies
(stochastic-block-model group size, graph degree, and
\mbox{FedAvg}-influence-derived dataset size) covering hierarchical,
decentralised, and cross-silo deployments respectively. We evaluate
\Fulcrum on three federated benchmarks --- Fed-ISIC2019 dermoscopy,
Fed-Heart-Disease tabular, and a synthetic \mbox{CIFAR-10} setup with
parametric topology--data coupling --- spanning star, hierarchical, line,
and ring topologies. The defense empirically dominates uniform
\mbox{DP-SGD} on the privacy bound at every utility budget tested,
costs no measurable test accuracy (paired $t$-tests give $p > 0.48$
on every setting), and bounds the parameter channel of the \TADI
attack across all configurations. The
prior-coupling term's empirical realisability is regime-dependent:
when the adversary's shadow training prior matches the target's
deployment prior, the bound is empirically attained; under the
public-proxy threat model with cross-silo benchmarks, the bound is
conservative in a deployment-favourable direction. The framework
provides a deployable defense consistent with its threat model,
with a provable bound, a closed-form noise allocation, and an
empirical characterisation of when its bound is tight versus
conservative.
\end{abstract}
