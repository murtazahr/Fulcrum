\begin{abstract}
Federated learning protects raw data by sharing only model updates, yet the
communication topology that orchestrates those updates is itself an attack
surface that existing privacy mechanisms do not address. We study a passive
adversary who knows the topology and organisational labelling of clients,
observes parameter updates, and seeks to recover each client's sensitive-class
concentration. We introduce \TADI, a topology-aware distributional inference
attack with four channel ablations that isolate the marginal information value
of parameter observations, structural position, and organisational membership.
We prove a per-client mutual-information bound that decomposes additively into
a controllable \mbox{DP-SGD} mechanism term and an uncontrollable
prior-coupling floor, and derive the closed-form balanced min-max optimal
allocation \Fulcrum, which strictly dominates uniform \mbox{DP-SGD} whenever
leverage scores are non-uniform. Three principled proxies cover the canonical
deployment regimes: stochastic-block-model group size (hierarchical
\mbox{FL}), graph degree (decentralised \mbox{FL}), and
\mbox{FedAvg}-influence dataset size (cross-silo \mbox{FL}). On three
federated benchmarks (Fed-ISIC2019 dermoscopy, Fed-Heart-Disease tabular,
and synthetic \mbox{CIFAR-10} with parametric topology--data coupling), the
defense dominates uniform \mbox{DP-SGD} on the (proxy) privacy bound at every
tested budget under the leverage proxy appropriate to each deployment regime,
exhibits utility equivalence to uniform allocation within a sub-percentage-point
margin (paired equivalence tests on per-seed accuracy), and bounds the \TADI
parameter channel across all configurations.
The prior-coupling term is empirically attained when adversary and target
priors match, and conservative in a deployment-favourable direction when only
public-proxy data is available.
\end{abstract}
